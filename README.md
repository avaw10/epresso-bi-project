# E-Presso BI Analyst (starter)

## What this application does
This application was created to help E-Presso managers better understand their business performance through an interactive dashboard and AI Assistant. Users can explore revenue, customer satisfaction, and sales trends by filtering the data and viewing different charts. We also created an AI Assistant that allows users to ask business questions and receive answers based on actual calculations from the data. The goal was to make the information easy to understand while ensuring the calculations are accurate and update when the data changes.

## How to use it
The application has three main sections:

### Data and Profile

This section allows users to upload and review the E-Presso datasets. Users can view information about each table, including its rows, columns, data types, missing values, and duplicate records.

Users can upload updated versions of the datasets without changing the application's Python code.

If an uploaded Excel workbook contains the `Sales`, `Products`, and `Categories` sheets, the application uses those sheets for the corresponding merges. A CSV replacement updates only the selected table while the remaining tables use their existing data.

### Dashboard

The Dashboard allows users to explore E-Presso's business performance through three key metrics:

- Total Revenue
- Average Store Customer Satisfaction
- Year-over-Year Revenue Growth

The Dashboard also includes four visualizations:

- Revenue by Year
- Revenue by Market
- Satisfaction by Market
- Monthly Revenue Percentage

Users can filter the data by Year, Month, Category, Store Type, Market, and State.

The metrics and charts automatically update based on the selected filters. If a filter combination does not have any matching sales records, the application displays a message rather than showing incorrect results.

### AI Assistant

The AI Assistant allows users to ask questions about E-Presso's performance without having to manually calculate the results.

For example, users can ask:

- What was total revenue in 2029?
- Which market generated the most revenue in 2029?
- Which stores experienced a revenue decline?
- What was the average customer satisfaction score in 2026?
- How did customer satisfaction change from 2026 to 2029?

The Assistant uses the full uploaded dataset, regardless of the filters selected on the Dashboard. Users should include a specific year or market in their questions when they want more specific results.

## Data Model

I used five E-Presso tables to build the application: Sales, Products, Categories, Stores, and Survey Results.

The Sales table contains revenue information, while the other tables provide additional information about products, categories, stores, and customer satisfaction.

### Grain Statements

The grain of a table describes what one row represents.

| Table | Source File | One Row = | Key Column | Rows | Contains Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sales | Sales in Support Sales | Monthly revenue generated by a specific product and category | Sales ID | 25,200 | Yes |
| Products | Products in Support Sales | One product identified by its name and ID | Product ID | 20 | No |
| Categories | Categories in Support Sales | One product category identified by its name and ID | Category ID | 5 | No |
| Stores | Stores | One store and its associated information | ID | 21 | No |
| Survey Results | Survey Results in Support Survey | One survey response associated with a store and survey ID | Survey ID | 107 | No |

### Merge Checks

I used left joins to combine the Sales table with the Products, Categories, and Stores tables.

To make sure the merges were accurate, I compared the number of rows and total revenue before and after each merge.

| Merge | Rows Before | Rows After | Revenue Sum Before | Revenue Sum After | Explanation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sales and Products by Product ID | 25,200 | 25,200 | $42,174,440.00 | $42,174,440.00 | Left join using Product ID |
| Sales and Categories by Category ID | 25,200 | 25,200 | $42,174,440.00 | $42,174,440.00 | Left join using Category ID |
| Sales and Stores by Store ID | 25,200 | 25,200 | $42,174,440.00 | $42,174,440.00 | Left join using the Sales Store ID |

All three merges maintained the original row count and revenue total. This confirmed that the merges did not duplicate or remove sales revenue.

I kept the Survey Results separate from the sales transactions because customer satisfaction is measured at the store level. Joining survey responses directly to individual sales could duplicate survey information and create misleading results.

## Metric definitions
### Total Revenue

Total revenue is calculated by adding together the revenue from the relevant sales records.

Revenue represents the money generated from sales. It does not represent profit because expenses are not included.

### Average Store Customer Satisfaction

Customer satisfaction is calculated using the average score from the available survey responses.

Scores are measured on a five-point scale.

The survey data represents store-level customer satisfaction rather than satisfaction with specific products or categories.

### Year-over-Year Revenue Growth

Year-over-year revenue growth measures how much revenue increased or decreased between two consecutive years.

Formula:

`((Current Year Revenue - Previous Year Revenue) / Previous Year Revenue) * 100`

If two consecutive years are not available in the selected data, the application displays N/A rather than calculating an inaccurate comparison.

### Monthly Revenue Percentage

Monthly revenue percentage shows how much each month contributes to the total revenue for the relevant period.

This helps identify patterns in sales throughout the year.

## Assistant
## AI Assistant

We created a live language-model-powered analytical assistant using OpenAI and approved Python analytical functions.

The AI model interprets the user's question and determines which function or functions are needed. Python then performs the calculations using the currently loaded E-Presso data.

The Assistant does not independently invent numerical results.

### Analytical Functions

The Assistant uses approved functions to perform calculations such as:

- Calculating total revenue for a selected year
- Grouping revenue by year or market
- Comparing store performance and identifying revenue declines
- Identifying stores with the greatest revenue increases or declines
- Calculating average customer satisfaction for a selected year
- Comparing customer satisfaction between two years
- Checking data quality and identifying potential issues

The Assistant can use multiple functions when a question requires more than one calculation.

### Assistant Responses

The Assistant provides:

- **Scope:** The data and time period included in the calculation.
- **Findings:** The numerical results from the analytical functions.
- **Interpretation:** An explanation of what those results mean.
- **Next Steps:** Additional analysis or information that may be helpful.
- **Tool-Call Log:** The functions and results used to answer the question.

### Assistant Restrictions

The Assistant is limited to approved analytical functions.

It cannot:

- Invent revenue or satisfaction numbers.
- Modify the uploaded datasets.
- Execute arbitrary AI-generated Python code.
- Calculate profitability without expense information.
- Claim that a relationship between two variables proves causation.
- Provide conclusions that are not supported by the available data.

If a question cannot be reliably answered, the Assistant explains the limitation rather than guessing.

## Validation
I completed many tests to make sure the application was calculating information accurately.

### Power BI Comparison

I compared the Streamlit application results against my original E-Presso Power BI assignment.

The comparisons included:

1. Total revenue across the five-year period.
2. Website revenue versus brick-and-mortar revenue.
3. Revenue trends by year.
4. Monthly revenue patterns.
5. An additional business performance measure.
6. A second additional business performance measure.

I also independently checked a total revenue calculation on excel to confirm that the results were accurate.

### $1,000 Modified-Data Test

I tested the application by creating a separate copy of the E-Presso dataset and increasing one revenue value by exactly $1,000.

I then uploaded the modified data and checked that:

- Total revenue increased by exactly $1,000.
- The relevant chart reflected the change.
- The appropriate filtered results updated.
- The AI Assistant used the updated data.
- No changes to the Python code were needed.

After completing the test, I restored the original dataset.

This test helped confirm that the application calculates results from the current data rather than relying on hardcoded values.

### Automated Testing

I created a `tests/` folder containing automated Python tests using `pytest`.

The tests use small sample datasets with answers that can be calculated by hand. This allows us to check whether individual analytical functions work correctly.

The automated tests cover:

- Total revenue calculations and filtering.
- Grouped revenue calculations.
- Missing and non-numeric values.
- Data-quality reporting.
- Merge accuracy and duplicate records.
- Missing required columns.
- Invalid filters and unexpected arguments.
- Unsupported questions.

There are also tests for detecting duplicated records and dropped rows during merges, even when the total revenue does not change. Some of these tests are still on the data profile tab as quality checks

### AI Assistant Testing

In addition to automated tests, I manually tested the AI Assistant through the Streamlit application.

I tested different types of questions, including numerical calculations, comparisons, questions requiring multiple analytical functions, and questions that could not be answered reliably using the available data, using AI to ensure all necessary questions were being asked.

I also checked whether the Assistant selected the correct functions, returned accurate results, and explained limitations when necessary.

## Limitations
Although the application provides useful business information, there are limitations to what it can tell a manager.

- Revenue does not equal profit because the dataset does not include all operating expenses.
- Customer satisfaction is based on available survey responses and may not represent every customer.
- An increase or decrease in revenue does not explain why the change occurred.
- A relationship between customer satisfaction and revenue does not prove that one caused the other.
- Missing or incomplete data can affect calculations.
- The AI Assistant can only answer questions supported by the available data and approved analytical functions.
- The live AI Assistant requires an API connection, which may be affected by usage limits or service availability.

The application is intended to help managers understand performance and identify areas to investigate further, rather than automatically make business decisions.

## Secrets

The OpenAI API key is stored securely in Streamlit Secrets using the `.streamlit/secrets.toml` file. This keeps sensitive information separate from the application code.

The `secrets.toml` file should never be uploaded to GitHub or committed to the repository. It is stored in .gitignore file. Only the example configuration file should be included if needed.
